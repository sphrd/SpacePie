How it works

Scan, explore, free space

Three steps. Open the app and the first one has already started.

1

Scan

Your system drive is scanned as soon as the app opens, in the background, with progress and Cancel. Other drives, USB disks or any folder are scanned when you ask: select it and press Scan, or drag a folder onto the window.

Scanning the system drive…
2

Explore

Browse while it runs. Folders that expand in place, the views on the left and a pie chart or treemap show where the space went: biggest folders, largest files, file types, duplicates, installed apps and what changed since the last scan.

3

Free space

Follow the suggestions or collect items on the Delete list (always one click away: the amber button left of Delete permanently), review them together, then move them to the Recycle Bin / Trash or delete them permanently. A confirmation listing every item always comes first.

Fast scanning

A whole drive in seconds, and an instant restart

SpacePie uses the fastest way to read your disk that the operating system offers, keeps working in the background, and remembers what it found.

A whole drive in seconds

On Windows, run as administrator and it reads an NTFS drive's master file table directly: over a million files in about ten seconds. Otherwise, and on macOS, it scans folders in parallel with the operating system's fastest directory calls.

Starts right away, in the background

A start-up screen shows what is being loaded the moment you launch it. The system drive is scanned when the app opens, with progress and Cancel, and you can browse while it runs. Other drives and USB disks are listed and scanned when you ask.

Instant restart

When you close it, the scan of each drive is saved; the next start shows it at once (a whole system drive in under two seconds). On Windows as administrator it then reads only what changed, from the NTFS change journal (on macOS, from the file system's event history), instead of scanning again.

Live updates

Files that appear, change or go away update the results within seconds, without rescanning.

Size on disk or file size

One click to switch. Compressed files count what they really take, hard links count once, and cloud files that are not downloaded (OneDrive online-only, iCloud) count nothing and are never downloaded by the scan.

Nothing hidden

When folders could not be read (no permission), a slim line says how many and lists them, instead of quietly leaving them out of the totals; folders the system closes to every app are not mentioned once the app has full access. Any text cut off with … shows in full on hover, and every list sorts by any column.

Seeing where the space went

Every view you need, one click away

The chart always shows the open list, as a pie chart or a treemap, and hovering a slice highlights its row and the other way round. Every item of at least 2 % of the total gets a slice of its own, in true proportion; smaller items together form one grey slice.

Pie or treemap

A pie chart with subfolders in two outer rings, or a treemap of rectangles sized by space with folders nested inside. One click to switch; the choice is remembered.

Knows what it is

Well-known folders and files (the Windows component store, hiberfil.sys, browser and developer caches, iPhone backups, Xcode data, node_modules…) are named, with whether they can go and where to clean them up properly.

Back, Forward and a path bar

Go back to the folders you opened (also with the mouse side buttons) and jump to any parent from the path above the list.

The views

Contents

The open folder's subfolders and files with size, share of the parent, change since the last scan, counts and dates, as a tree-list: expand folders in place to compare what is inside. Above every drive, This PC lists all drives with their used and free space. Click a part of the chart to open it, click the centre to go back up.

Suggestions

How much can be freed, in large print, and what: the Recycle Bin / Trash on every drive, temporary files, user and system caches and logs, developer caches, the node_modules folders of your own projects, mail attachments, unfinished downloads, unused disk images, old updates, leftovers of removed apps and more, each with what deleting it means. Check exactly what you want; safe items start checked, items to review first start unchecked. Never suggest this hides an item for good.

Apps

Every installed app with its own logo and its real size on disk. Expand an app to see everything that belongs to it: program folder, settings and caches, shared data, shortcuts and, on Windows, registry keys. Uninstall… removes the app and asks to delete whatever is left. Not opened for 6 months lists the apps you no longer use.

System

Windows: update leftovers in the component store, restore points, hibernation and page files, WSL / Docker virtual disks, a registry check of broken entries, and every drive's free space and health. macOS: Time Machine local snapshots, purgeable space, iPhone / iPad backups and every volume's health (SMART status).

Files

The largest files, with a name search (*.iso, invoice) and filters by type, size and date: not changed, or not opened, for months or years. Search the open folder or everything scanned at once. Instant, from memory.

File types

Space by extension or by category: videos, images, archives, disk images, installers and more.

Duplicates

Files with identical content. Select extra copies keeps the newest of each group, or Replace with links keeps every file where it is but stores the data once (Windows hard links, APFS clones on macOS). A repeat search only reads new or changed files.

Changes

Which folders grew or shrank since the previous scan, or between any two scans: a timeline shows the used space at every scan, and one small copy per day is kept to compare with.

Delete list

Always one click away: the amber button left of Delete permanently. Collect items from any view, review them together, then Move to Recycle Bin / Trash (can be undone) or Delete permanently (frees the space now).

Safe cleaning

Free space without surprises

Nothing is deleted without a confirmation that shows the total size and every single item. SpacePie tells you what deleting means before you do it.

Before you delete

  • Confirmation first, always, with a warning sign, the total size and every item in a scrollable list, plus a warning for Windows / Program Files content. Copy list copies it.
  • Synced folders: a warning when an item is in OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive, iCloud Drive and similar, because deleting it there deletes it in the cloud and on your other devices too.
  • Recycle Bin limits: on Windows it says when an item can't go to the Recycle Bin (too large, the bin turned off, or a USB stick without one) and moves only the others.
  • Uninstall leftovers you can trust: each one says how it was matched (exact, same developer, or name only). Name-only matches are shown but never selected for you. Registry keys are saved to a .reg backup first.

While and after it deletes

  • Recycle Bin / Trash or permanent: moving to the Recycle Bin / Trash can be undone; deleting permanently frees the space now.
  • Watch it go: a progress strip counts the files removed and the space freed, and Stop halts it after the current file. Items leave the list as they are deleted, and the chart follows.
  • Nothing silently skipped: what could not be deleted (in use, access denied) is listed afterwards with the reason for each item; the delete list keeps what is left, with its real size.
  • Only what you picked: links are never followed, very long paths work, caches are emptied and files in use are skipped.

Delete permanently cannot be undone. Use Move to Recycle Bin / Trash if you may want something back.

History and saved scans

See what changed, keep a record

SpacePie remembers earlier scans so you can see where new space went, and lets you save a scan to look at later or send to someone.

Changes over time

Compare the previous scan or any two scans. A timeline shows the used space at every scan; the list shows which folders grew or shrank.

Save and open scans

Export a folder as an HTML report, as CSV, or as a .spacepie scan file that Open scan… shows again later, read-only, next to the drives.

Preview and properties

Space shows a preview (Quick Look on macOS); Alt+Enter opens the Windows Properties dialog (Get Info, ⌘I, on macOS).

Updates itself

It checks for a new version at startup and installs it in a click. About lists the keyboard shortcuts and how to get the fastest, most complete scans.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your computer

No account, no telemetry, no analytics and no advertising. SpacePie runs on your computer and keeps what it learns there.

What it reads and sends

  • Names, sizes and dates of what you scan. It never reads the contents of a file, except to compare duplicates (only content hashes are kept) and the first 64 KB of a text file you choose to preview on Windows.
  • Online-only cloud files are never opened, previewed or downloaded.
  • The only network requests go to GitHub, for updates: at start and when you press Check for updates, and the release file when you press Update now. Nothing about your files or your computer is sent.

What it keeps and changes

  • Kept locally only: scan history for the Changes timeline, the full scan of the most recently scanned drives for the instant restart, duplicate hashes, the list of installed apps and the suggestions you chose to ignore, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\SpacePie or ~/Library/Application Support/SpacePie. Delete the folder to remove all of it.
  • Changes nothing unless you ask: deleting, uninstalling, the System view's clean-up actions, replacing duplicates with links, or turning "Always start as administrator" on or off.
  • Check it yourself: every web address and network call is in the open source. Read PRIVACY.md.

Accuracy and speed

Fast, and checked against the operating system's own tools

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is SpacePie free?

Yes. SpacePie is free and open source under the MIT licence. There is no account and no advertising.

Is it safe? What does it send?

Nothing about your files or your computer is sent. The only network requests go to GitHub, to check for and download updates; GitHub sees your IP address and the app's version, like for any download.

It changes nothing unless you ask, and every delete starts with a confirmation that lists every item. Move to Recycle Bin / Trash can be undone; Delete permanently cannot. Details: PRIVACY.md.

Why does Windows SmartScreen or macOS warn me on first start?

The app is not code-signed yet, so both systems show a warning the first time.

Windows: click More info, then Run anyway. macOS: right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again (or click Open Anyway in System Settings > Privacy & Security).

To check the download, compare its SHA-256 with the one in the release notes, for example with Get-FileHash .\SpacePie.exe on Windows.

Does it need installation?

Windows: no. Download SpacePie.exe and run it. For the fastest whole-drive scans, click Run as administrator in the toolbar.

macOS: open SpacePie-macOS.dmg and drag SpacePie onto Applications. On first start it asks for Full Disk Access; turn it on in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access, then reopen the app.

Which systems does it run on?

Windows 10 and Windows 11, and macOS 11 or later, natively on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

How do I update?

Use Check for updates inside the app. It also checks for a new version at startup and installs it in a click.

Is it open source?

Yes, under the MIT licence. The source is on GitHub; every push is built and tested on Windows and macOS by GitHub Actions. On Windows it builds with the C# compiler built into Windows, no Visual Studio or SDK needed.
